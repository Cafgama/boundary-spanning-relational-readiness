# Finite-Window Scaling Near the Admission Boundary

**Status:** pre-simulation analytical prediction for E1.

## 1. Purpose

The fluid theory predicts a deterministic first-exhaustion boundary

`chi = Omega Lambda = 1`.

Finite capacity windows smooth this boundary because realized actor demand fluctuates around its expectation. This note derives the expected scale of that smoothing before examining E1 results.

## 2. Marginal actor demand

Before any endpoint is exhausted, an actor with responsibility share `p_i` is selected on its module side with marginal probability `p_i` on each of the `D` attempted interactions. Therefore its pre-exhaustion demand count satisfies

`N_i ~ Binomial(D, p_i)`

marginally, even though actor counts within a module are jointly multinomial.

Its integer capacity is approximately

`c_i = C x_i`.

With

`D = Omega C`,

the mean-capacity difference is

`E[N_i] - c_i = C (Omega p_i - x_i)`.

The binomial standard deviation is

`sigma_i = sqrt[Omega C p_i (1-p_i)]`.

Hence the standardized local overload distance is

`Z_i = sqrt(C) * (Omega p_i - x_i) / sqrt[Omega p_i (1-p_i)]`.

## 3. Critical carrier and chi scaling

Let `i*` be a unique first-exhausting carrier with

`p*/x* = Lambda`.

Its fluid threshold is

`Omega_c = x*/p* = 1/Lambda`.

Since

`chi = Omega Lambda`,

we have

`Omega p* - x* = x* (chi - 1)`.

Near the threshold, substituting `Omega ~= Omega_c` in the denominator gives

`Z* ~= sqrt[C x*/(1-p*)] * (chi - 1)`.

Therefore stochastic curves observed at different capacity scales should change over a boundary layer whose width obeys

`Delta chi ~ sqrt[(1-p*)/(C x*)]`.

In particular,

`Delta chi proportional to C^(-1/2)`.

This is a finite-window analogue of finite-size rounding.

## 4. Blocking amplitude at the threshold

At `chi=1`, the controlling carrier has mean demand approximately equal to its capacity. Under a normal approximation, the expected positive excess demand is of order its standard deviation,

`E[(N_i-c_i)_+] = O(sqrt(C))`.

Because the number of attempted demands is `D=O(C)`, the blocked fraction generated by threshold fluctuations is expected to scale as

`f_blocked(chi=1) = O(C^(-1/2))`.

The exact prefactor can differ because admission is bilateral and because multiple actors can share the first-exhaustion threshold. The exponent `1/2`, rather than the prefactor, is the primary screening prediction.

## 5. E1 predictions

E1 should therefore examine whether:

1. the deviation from the fluid curve contracts as `C` increases;
2. the width of the stochastic crossover around `chi=1` is consistent with `C^(-1/2)`;
3. the mean blocked fraction at the theoretical onset declines approximately as `C^(-1/2)`;
4. plotting local boundary distance against `sqrt(C)*(chi-1)` improves cross-scale alignment near first exhaustion.

These are pre-specified predictions. A formal scaling claim should only be made after additional capacity scales and replication-convergence checks if the screening evidence supports them.

## 6. Guardrail

This derivation concerns the **first-exhaustion neighborhood**. It does not imply that the entire post-onset blocking curve collapses under `sqrt(C)*(chi-1)` or under `chi` alone. Once carriers exhaust, the full ordered threshold structure `{x_i/p_i}` enters the fluid dynamics.
